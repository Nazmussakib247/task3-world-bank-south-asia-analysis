In this project, I analyzed World Bank World Development Indicators for Bangladesh, India, Nepal, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka from 2000 through 2024. I joined two country-year series: GDP per capita in constant 2015 US dollars and life expectancy at birth in years. I checked the downloaded data for missing values, duplicate country-year records, non-positive GDP values, and implausible life-expectancy values. The resulting panel contains 125 observations, with complete coverage for both indicators across the five countries and 25 years. I compared country trends, calculated endpoint changes and annualized GDP-per-capita growth, calculated pooled medians and interquartile ranges, and examined the association between the indicators. In the pooled panel, median GDP per capita was $1,265 and median life expectancy was 67.8 years. Across 2000–2024, GDP per capita changed as follows: Bangladesh: 212.8% GDP per capita change, life expectancy +12.9 years; India: 212.8% GDP per capita change, life expectancy +9.5 years; Nepal: 115.6% GDP per capita change, life expectancy +8.0 years; Pakistan: 55.8% GDP per capita change, life expectancy +5.9 years; Sri Lanka: 114.6% GDP per capita change, life expectancy +6.5 years. In 2024, Sri Lanka had the highest GDP per capita among these five countries, while Sri Lanka had the highest life expectancy. The pooled Pearson correlation across the country-year observations was 0.84; I treat it as descriptive because observations from different countries and years are not independent, and it does not show that income causes longer lives. The report includes four labeled charts, data-quality checks, methods, findings, limitations, source citations, and steps to reproduce the analysis. I saved the source data, cleaned dataset, runnable Python script, Jupyter notebook, and summary outputs in the accompanying GitHub project.
